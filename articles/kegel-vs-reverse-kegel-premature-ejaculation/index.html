Kegel vs Reverse Kegel for Premature Ejaculation

Reviewed November 23, 2026By Sergei ProstokishynEvidence-aware guide

In the Kegel vs reverse Kegel for premature ejaculation debate, the honest answer is that you usually need both: Kegels train contraction and control, while reverse Kegels train release. Regular Kegels have a small body of research in premature ejaculation; reverse Kegels have almost none directly, though relaxation is a recognized part of pelvic floor therapy.

Short answer: If your pelvic floor feels weak or hard to find, start with Kegels plus full relaxation. If it feels tight, painful or constantly clenched, start with relaxation and reverse Kegels, and get assessed. Neither is a guaranteed fix for PE.

What Is the Difference?

A Kegel is a voluntary contraction of the pelvic floor: a lift and squeeze, as if stopping the flow of urine or holding in gas. A reverse Kegel is the opposite movement: a deliberate, gentle release and lengthening of the pelvic floor, usually paired with a slow inhale. It is not straining or pushing as if on the toilet.

For a step-by-step guide to the release, read Reverse Kegels for Men. If you cannot find the muscles at all, start with How to Find Your Pelvic Floor Muscles.

What the Evidence Says About Kegels for Premature Ejaculation

Studies show that pelvic floor rehabilitation may help some men with premature ejaculation. In the 2014 study by Pastore and colleagues, most of the 40 men with lifelong PE gained some control after a 12-week program. But that program included biofeedback and electrical stimulation, not just home exercises.

A 2019 systematic review found pelvic floor training looked helpful for PE and erectile dysfunction, but protocols varied widely. A 2026 meta-analysis was more cautious: pelvic floor and sphincter-control training showed a smaller effect on latency than the comparison treatments in the included studies. The full picture is in Do Kegels Help With Premature Ejaculation?.

Does Reverse Kegel Help With Premature Ejaculation?

There are no good trials testing reverse Kegels alone for premature ejaculation. Claims that they are "the real cure" come from forums, not research.

The evidence that exists is indirect:

  • A 2023 study found that adding sphincter control training, which focuses on awareness and relaxation of the pelvic sphincters, to the stop-start technique worked better than stop-start alone. It was not randomized, and it is not identical to reverse Kegels.
  • Relaxation-based pelvic floor therapy is used for men with pelvic pain and ejaculation-related pain.
  • Tension tends to raise arousal, which is one reason relaxation is often taught as they approach the point of no return.

So reverse Kegels make sense as a relaxation skill, especially for a tight pelvic floor, but there is no evidence that they work better than Kegels for PE in general.

Which Should You Start With? Weak vs Tight Pelvic Floor

Only an assessment can tell for sure, but these patterns are a rough guide.

Signs your pelvic floor may be under-active (start with Kegels plus full release):

  • You struggle to feel or hold a contraction.
  • You have dribbling after urinating or leaks with effort.

Signs it may be overactive or tight (start with relaxation and reverse Kegels):

  • Pelvic, perineal or testicular pain, or pain after ejaculation.
  • Difficulty starting urination or a weak stream.
  • A constant clenched feeling, or symptoms that get worse after Kegels.

These symptoms can have other causes, so they are a reason to see a clinician, not a self-diagnosis. Cleveland Clinic describes physical therapy as central to treating a diagnosed hypertonic pelvic floor.

Reverse Kegel Exercises for Premature Ejaculation: A Balanced Routine

Without pain, a balanced session covers both directions:

  1. Release first: 3–5 slow breaths, letting the pelvic floor soften on each inhale.
  2. Kegels: a few short squeezes and a few longer holds, each followed by full relaxation at least as long as the squeeze.
  3. Release again: 3–5 slow breaths to finish.
  4. Apply it: during stop-start practice, use the release when arousal reaches 6–7.

Do not hold your breath or bear down. Stop if anything hurts. For in-the-moment technique, read How to Control Ejaculation With Your Pelvic Floor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does reverse Kegel help with premature ejaculation?

It may help some men, especially those with a tight or overactive pelvic floor, by teaching them to relax instead of clench. There is very little direct research on reverse Kegels for PE.

Are Kegels or reverse Kegels better for premature ejaculation?

Neither has been shown to be better for everyone. Kegels have more research, mostly from supervised programs; reverse Kegels fit best when tension or pain is involved. Many men train both.

Can Kegels make premature ejaculation worse?

Possibly, if your pelvic floor is already overactive. If symptoms worsen or you develop pain after Kegels, stop and get assessed.

Do reverse Kegels help you last longer?

They may help indirectly by reducing tension as arousal rises. They are not proven to increase time to ejaculation on their own.

This article is for general education and is not medical advice. If you have premature ejaculation with pain, urinary symptoms or erection problems, see a urologist, sexual medicine clinician or pelvic floor physical therapist.
