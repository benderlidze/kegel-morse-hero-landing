Kegel Exercises to Last Longer in Bed: 4-Week Plan for Men

Reviewed November 23, 2026By Sergei ProstokishynEvidence-aware guide

Kegel exercises to last longer in bed for men work best as a skill program, not a squeeze-as-hard-as-you-can challenge. The goal is to feel the pelvic floor, contract it on purpose, and, just as important, let it fully relax. Small studies suggest that pelvic floor training may improve ejaculatory control for some men, especially as part of a structured program.

Short answer: Train quick squeezes, slow holds, and full relaxation for a few minutes a day, moving from lying down to sitting to standing over four weeks. Expect weeks to months, not days, and see a clinician if you have pain or if short lasting time is causing real distress.

Do Kegels Make You Last Longer in Bed?

They may, for some men. The best-known study, by Pastore and colleagues in 2014, followed 40 men with lifelong premature ejaculation through a 12-week pelvic floor rehabilitation program. The authors reported that 33 of the 40 gained control over the ejaculatory reflex by the end of the program.

That result is encouraging, but it needs context:

  • The study was small.
  • The program was supervised and included biofeedback and electrical stimulation, not only home Kegels.
  • Reviews since then, including Myers and colleagues in 2019, found the evidence promising but varied, with no single agreed protocol.

So the honest answer is "possibly, with patience." For the full evidence picture, read Do Kegels Help With Premature Ejaculation?.

How the Pelvic Floor Connects to Ejaculatory Control

Two pelvic floor muscles, the bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus, take part in erection rigidity and in the rhythmic contractions of ejaculation. Training them may help in three ways:

  1. Awareness. Practice makes pelvic tension during arousal easier to notice earlier.
  2. Voluntary control. A muscle you can switch on and off on purpose is easier to manage than one that only reacts.
  3. Relaxation. Being able to soften a clenched pelvic floor on demand is part of control.

This is a proposed mechanism, not proof that stronger muscles alone make you last longer. That is why the plan below gives relaxation the same weight as squeezing.

Before You Start: Find the Right Muscle

The target is the muscle you would use to stop the flow of urine or hold back gas. When you contract it, the base of the penis should draw slightly in and the testicles may lift a little. Your buttocks, thighs, and belly should stay mostly soft, and you should keep breathing.

Do not practice by stopping urine regularly; use it at most once to identify the feeling. If you are not sure you have it, start with How to Find Your Pelvic Floor Muscles.

The 4-Week Plan: Kegel Exercises to Last Longer in Bed

Do one session twice a day. Each session takes about four to six minutes. Rest at least as long as each hold, and breathe out gently as you squeeze.

Many NHS pelvic floor leaflets for men suggest building toward slow holds of up to 10 seconds and sets of fast contractions spread through the day. This plan reaches that range gradually. If a level feels shaky, repeat the week instead of moving on.

Week 1: Learn the Squeeze and the Release

Lying down removes gravity, so the muscle is easier to feel. Squeeze gently for 3 seconds, then let go completely for at least 3 seconds. Then do 5 quick squeezes, about one second on and one second off. Finish with five slow breaths, letting the area between the scrotum and anus soften on each exhale.

Week 2: Sitting and Longer Holds

Sit upright on a firm chair. Hold for 5 seconds, rest for 5 seconds. Add reverse Kegels: breathe in slowly, let the belly expand, and allow the pelvic floor to drop and lengthen without pushing hard. Read Reverse Kegels for Men for the technique.

Week 3: Standing

Standing is harder because the muscles work against gravity. Keep the holds smooth, not maximal. A 60 to 70 percent effort that you can hold steadily is better than a hard clench that fades after two seconds.

Week 4: Control Drills

Mix positions across the day. Add a simple control drill: squeeze for 3 seconds, release halfway, hold the half-squeeze for 2 seconds, then release fully. This trains the fine grading of tension, not just on and off. Quick vs. slow work is explained in more detail in Quick vs. Slow Kegels.

Best Kegel Exercises for Men to Last Longer in Bed

There is no single best exercise. A balanced routine uses three types:

  • Slow holds build endurance and steady control.
  • Quick squeezes train fast, deliberate activation.
  • Reverse Kegels and relaxed breathing teach you to release tension instead of carrying it into sex.

Men often skip the third type, yet a pelvic floor that never fully lets go may contribute to pain or urinary symptoms.

How Long Until You Notice a Difference?

Expect gradual change. In the Pastore program, the rehabilitation phase lasted 12 weeks. General pelvic floor training guidance usually talks about several weeks to a few months before a noticeable change in strength or control.

A realistic timeline looks like this:

  1. Weeks 1 to 2: You find the muscle more easily and can release it on purpose.
  2. Weeks 3 to 6: Holds feel steadier; you may notice pelvic tension earlier during arousal.
  3. Weeks 6 to 12: Some men notice better control during sex. Others notice little change, which is a reason to get assessed rather than squeeze harder.

For more on timing, see How Long Do Kegels Take to Work for Men?.

When Not to Do This Plan

Stop strengthening and get assessed if you have:

  • Pelvic, testicular, or penile pain, or pain after ejaculation.
  • Difficulty starting urine, a weak stream, or urgency that gets worse.
  • A constant gripping or clenched feeling in the pelvis.
  • Symptoms that worsen after sessions.

These can point to an over-tight pelvic floor, where more squeezing may make things worse and relaxation work is usually the priority.

Also see a clinician if short lasting time is new, came with erection problems, or is causing distress. Erectile dysfunction, prostatitis, thyroid problems, medications, and anxiety can all play a role.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Kegels make you last longer as a man?

They may help some men gain control, based on small studies of supervised pelvic floor rehabilitation. They are not a guaranteed fix, and results vary widely.

Do reverse Kegels help you last longer?

Reverse Kegels train relaxation, which may help men whose pelvic floor stays tense during arousal. Evidence specific to lasting longer is limited, so use them as part of a balanced routine rather than on their own.

Can I do a Kegel right before ejaculation to delay it?

Some men report that a deliberate squeeze or a deliberate release helps them pause, but there is no standardized, well-tested in-the-moment technique. Slowing down, as in the stop-start technique, has a longer track record.

How many Kegels should I do to last longer in bed?

There is no proven number. Short, regular sessions with full rest between contractions are more sensible than very high rep counts.
