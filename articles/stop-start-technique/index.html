The Stop-Start Technique: How to Do It, Step by Step

Reviewed November 23, 2026By Sergei ProstokishynEvidence-aware guide

The stop-start technique for men is a simple behavioral exercise: you stop sexual stimulation just before the point of no return, let arousal drop, then start again. Repeated over weeks, it may help you recognize your arousal levels earlier and gain more control over when ejaculation happens.

Short answer: Practice alone first, pausing three or four times before you finish, then bring the same pauses into sex with a partner. It takes regular practice, and the evidence is encouraging but not strong.

What Is the Stop-Start Technique?

The method was described by James Semans in 1956 and later adapted by Masters and Johnson into the squeeze technique. The NHS page on ejaculation problems describes both and notes that they need a lot of practice.

The goal is not to "hold it in" by force, but to learn where your arousal is and back off early enough that the urge fades on its own.

Stop-Start Technique for Men: Solo Practice Step by Step

Start alone, without the pressure of a partner. Use a lubricant and a relaxed, private setting.

  1. Rate your arousal. Use a simple 0–10 scale: 0 is no arousal, 10 is ejaculation. The "point of no return" usually sits around 8–9.
  2. Stimulate yourself slowly and notice your breathing, belly, thighs and pelvic floor.
  3. Stop at about 7. Take your hand away completely. Do not wait until you are at 9.
  4. Let arousal drop to about 4 or 5. Give it at least 30 seconds, longer if needed. Breathe slowly and let your pelvic floor and buttocks soften.
  5. Start again. Repeat the cycle three or four times.
  6. On the last round, let yourself finish. The session should end with ejaculation, not with frustration.

Aim for two or three sessions a week. If you go past the point of no return, that is normal early on. Note it and stop a little earlier next time.

Common mistakes

  • Stopping too late, at 9 instead of 7.
  • Clenching or holding your breath during the pause.
  • Restarting before arousal has really dropped.
  • Treating one bad session as failure.

The Squeeze Technique Variation

The squeeze technique adds pressure during the pause. Just before the point of no return, you (or a partner) firmly squeeze the head of the penis, where it meets the shaft, for 10 to 20 seconds until the urge passes, then wait about 30 seconds before continuing. That is the version the NHS describes.

Some men find the squeeze helps; others find the plain pause less distracting. Keep the one you can repeat comfortably.

Using the Stop-Start Technique With a Partner

Move on once you can pause and restart reliably alone, usually after a few weeks.

  1. Talk first. Explain what you are practicing and agree on a word or signal for "pause."
  2. Start with manual stimulation from your partner, using the same 0–10 scale.
  3. Move to intercourse with little movement. Positions where your partner is on top make it easier to stop, as the NHS suggests.
  4. Pause by stopping movement, not necessarily withdrawing. Breathe, soften the pelvic floor, then resume.
  5. Gradually add more movement as your control improves.

How to Combine It With Pelvic Floor Relaxation and Kegels

During the pause, many men instinctively clench everything. A softer approach usually works better: exhale slowly and let the pelvic floor release, as in a reverse Kegel. Tension tends to push arousal up, while relaxation helps it settle.

Does the Stop-Start Technique Work? What the Evidence Says

Probably for some men, but the evidence is modest.

  • A 2015 systematic review by Cooper and colleagues found ten randomized trials of behavioral therapies, including stop-start and squeeze. Results were mixed and the risk of bias was unclear. Combining behavioral therapy with medication worked better than medication alone, but the authors called for more trials.
  • A 2023 study by Doğan and Keçe reported that stop-start helped men with premature ejaculation and that adding sphincter control training, which focuses on awareness and relaxation of the pelvic sphincters, worked better than stop-start alone. It was not a randomized trial.
  • Pelvic floor rehabilitation has its own small evidence base, reviewed in Do Kegels Help With Premature Ejaculation?.

Mayo Clinic lists behavioral techniques alongside pelvic floor exercises, counseling and medication. A clinician can help you combine them.

When to See a Doctor

See a urologist or sexual medicine clinician if premature ejaculation is new, comes with erection problems, pain, urinary symptoms, or distress, or if several weeks of practice change nothing. Acquired PE can be linked to erectile dysfunction, prostatitis, thyroid problems or anxiety.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the stop-start technique take to work?

Expect several weeks of regular practice. There is no fixed timeline, and progress is usually gradual.

Is the stop-start technique better than the squeeze technique?

Neither has been shown to be clearly better. The squeeze adds pressure to the head of the penis during the pause; plain stop-start relies only on stopping. Use whichever you can do consistently.

Can I do the stop-start technique without a partner?

Yes. Solo practice is the recommended starting point, because it lets you learn your arousal levels without pressure.

Does the stop-start technique help premature ejaculation permanently?

Some men keep the control they build, especially if they keep using pauses. Others need ongoing practice or additional treatment. No technique guarantees a permanent result.

This article is for general education and is not medical advice. If premature ejaculation is new, distressing or comes with other symptoms, see a urologist, sexual medicine clinician or pelvic floor physical therapist.
