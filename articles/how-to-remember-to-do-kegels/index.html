How to Remember to Do Kegels Every Day

Reviewed September 22, 2026By Kegel Morse HeroEvidence-aware guide

Kegels work. The problem is that almost nobody keeps doing them. You start strong on Monday, forget by Thursday, and a month later you're back at zero. If you want to know how to remember to do Kegels, the answer isn't more willpower. It's building the habit into things you already do.

Results usually show up after 6–8 weeks of consistent practice. That means consistency is the whole game. Here's how to make it automatic.

Anchor Kegels to an Existing Habit

Habit researchers call this "habit stacking": attach a new behavior to one you already do every day without thinking.

Good anchors for Kegels:

  • Waiting for coffee to brew
  • Red lights while driving
  • Brushing your teeth (2 minutes = one full set)
  • The first minute after you sit down at your desk
  • Every time you unlock your phone in the morning

Pick one anchor, not five. One anchor you actually use beats a perfect plan you forget.

Keep Sessions Tiny

A set of 10 squeezes takes under two minutes. If your plan feels like a workout, you'll skip it. If it feels like nothing, you'll do it. Start with one short set a day and add more only after a full week without missing.

Use Reminders That Don't Feel Like Nagging

A phone alarm labeled "KEGELS" works for about three days, then you start swiping it away. Better options:

  • A reminder with a neutral label only you understand
  • A reminder tied to a location (arriving at the office)
  • An app that turns the reminder into something you want to open

Track Your Streak

Seeing a chain of completed days is surprisingly motivating. Not wanting to break a 14-day streak is often what gets you through day 15. A calendar on the wall, a note on your phone, or an app—anything that shows the chain.

Make It a Game

Boredom kills Kegel routines faster than anything. Squeeze, hold, release, repeat—it's the same thing every day with nothing to show for it.

Gamified training changes that. Instead of counting in your head, you're hitting targets, completing levels, or—in the case of Morse code training—spelling out words with short and long squeezes. Your brain stays engaged, and sessions stop feeling like a chore. See Kegel Exercise Games for more on why this works.

Do Them in a Set Position First, Then Anywhere

Most people find Kegels easiest lying down at first. Once you're confident you're squeezing the right muscles, move to sitting, then standing. When you can do them anywhere, your opportunities to practice multiply—and you'll forget less often.

Don't Restart From Zero After a Missed Day

Missing one day doesn't break your progress. Missing one day and then deciding "I'll start fresh next Monday" does. The rule: never miss twice in a row.

The Simplest Daily Kegel Habit

If you want a starting template:

  • When: after you brush your teeth in the morning
  • What: one set of 10 squeezes, 3 seconds hold, 3 seconds release
  • Track: mark the day in an app or on a calendar
  • Upgrade: after 2 weeks, add a second set in the evening
