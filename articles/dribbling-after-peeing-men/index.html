Dribbling After Peeing? Why That Last Drop Happens

Reviewed September 28, 2026By Kegel Morse HeroEvidence-aware guide

You finish, wait, get dressed—and then a few drops escape. It is frustrating precisely because you thought you were done.

This is often called post-micturition dribble, or after-dribble. It can happen at different ages. The useful question is whether those drops are left in the outlet tube or whether you are having a broader problem emptying your bladder.

Why the Last Drop Waits Until You Move

A small amount of urine can remain in the urethra, the tube carrying urine out of the body. Movement then lets it escape. Pelvic floor muscles help clear that tube, which explains why they can be part of the solution.

The Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS guide explains this mechanism and a gentle clearing technique.

A Technique to Ask About

After urinating normally, wait a few seconds. With clean hands, place fingertips on the perineum, roughly three finger-widths behind the scrotum. Apply gentle pressure and move forward beneath the scrotum toward the base of the penis. Then clear the remaining drops normally.

This is sometimes called urethral milking. It should not hurt or involve force. If you have recently had surgery, ask your care team before trying it.

Where Kegels Can Help

Pelvic floor training can help men with after-dribble, according to University Hospitals Sussex. First, learn a controlled inward lift with normal breathing and a clear release afterward.

A continence nurse or physiotherapist can check the movement and suggest an appropriate routine. There is no reason to treat every toilet visit as an extra workout.

Our guide to finding your pelvic floor muscles can help you understand the movement before practicing.

The Mistake to Avoid: Training While Peeing

Repeatedly stopping and starting the urine stream is not a daily Kegel routine. It can interfere with normal emptying. Practice separately, and let the pelvic floor relax while you urinate.

If squeezing causes discomfort or you struggle to release, get advice before increasing repetitions. Pelvic floor relaxation is a different skill from squeezing harder.

When It Is More Than After-Dribble

Arrange an assessment if leakage comes with a weak stream, trouble getting started, or a repeated feeling that your bladder is not empty. Do not assume all leakage proves weak muscles.

NIDDK explains that retention can also involve blockage, impaired bladder contraction, or tight pelvic floor muscles.

If you cannot urinate, or have severe lower abdominal pain, seek urgent medical care.
