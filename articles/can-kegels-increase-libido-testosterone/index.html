Can Kegels Increase Libido or Testosterone?

Reviewed November 23, 2026By Sergei ProstokishynEvidence-aware guide

Can Kegels increase libido? There is no good evidence that Kegel exercises directly raise sex drive or testosterone. They are small muscle exercises for the pelvic floor, not a hormonal workout. They may still help your sex life indirectly through erection quality, control and confidence.

Short answer: No study shows that Kegels raise testosterone or libido directly. Sleep, weight, alcohol, stress, medicines, relationship factors and medical conditions matter far more. Kegels may help some men feel more confident and in control in bed.

Can Kegels Increase Testosterone?

Testosterone is made mainly in the testicles, under signals from the brain. Large-muscle exercise such as strength training places a different kind of load on the body than a few seconds of pelvic floor squeezing. We found no clinical trial showing that pelvic floor training changes testosterone levels.

You may see claims that Kegels "boost T" because they increase blood flow to the pelvis. That is a guess dressed up as a fact. Blood flow and hormone production are different things.

If you suspect low testosterone, the answer is a blood test, not more reps. Mayo Clinic lists symptoms of male hypogonadism such as low sex drive, erectile problems, fatigue and loss of muscle mass. A diagnosis needs symptoms plus properly timed blood tests.

Do Kegels Increase Sex Drive?

Libido is mostly driven by the brain, hormones, mood and context. Kegels do not act on those directly, and no good study shows a direct libido effect.

There is one realistic indirect route: if sex has been frustrating because of weak erections or finishing too fast, and pelvic floor training helps with that, you may feel more interested in sex. That is a confidence effect, not a hormonal one.

The NHS covers most of these causes of low sex drive. Regular aerobic and strength exercise also supports cardiovascular health, weight and mood, which is a better bet for your sex life than any single exercise.

Where Kegels May Help Indirectly

Kegels are not useless for sex. Their likely benefits are mechanical and behavioral:

  1. Erection quality. The pelvic floor muscles help maintain rigidity. A 2004 randomized trial found that pelvic floor exercises with biofeedback and lifestyle advice improved erectile function more than lifestyle advice alone. Newer reviews are mixed; see Kegels and erectile dysfunction.
  2. Ejaculation control. A 2019 systematic review concluded pelvic floor training appeared helpful for ED and premature ejaculation, but protocols varied widely. More in Do Kegels Help Premature Ejaculation?
  3. Body awareness and confidence. Knowing how to contract and fully relax these muscles can make you feel more in control, which can make sex feel less stressful.

What Kegels will not do: raise hormones, grow the penis or replace medical care for low testosterone. For the broader picture, read What Do Kegels Do for Men?

A Simple Starting Routine

If you want to try pelvic floor training for erection quality or control:

  1. Find the muscles by imagining stopping urine and holding in gas at the same time.
  2. Squeeze for 3 seconds, then relax fully for 5–6 seconds.
  3. Do 10 reps, once or twice a day.
  4. Add a few quick 1-second squeezes with full relaxation between them.
  5. Give it 6–12 weeks before judging results.

Stop and get assessed if you develop pelvic pain, pain after ejaculation or difficulty starting urine. An over-tight pelvic floor needs relaxation, not more squeezing. See How Many Kegels a Day for Men for volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Kegels increase libido?

Not directly. No good evidence shows Kegels raise sex drive. They may help indirectly if better erections or control make sex less stressful.

Can Kegels increase testosterone?

There is no evidence that Kegels raise testosterone. If you have low desire, fatigue and weaker erections together, ask a clinician about a testosterone blood test.

What do Kegels do for men sexually?

They may help some men with erection firmness and ejaculation control. Results vary, and they work best as part of a broader plan.

What raises libido naturally?

Enough sleep, less alcohol, managing stress, regular exercise, a healthy weight and a good relationship with your partner are the most practical starting points.

This article is for general education and is not medical advice. For low desire or suspected low testosterone, see a doctor, urologist or sexual medicine clinician; for pelvic floor symptoms, see a pelvic floor physical therapist.
