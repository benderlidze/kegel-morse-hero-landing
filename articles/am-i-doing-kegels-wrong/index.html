Am I Doing Kegels Wrong? 7 Signs and Fixes

Reviewed September 22, 2026By Kegel Morse HeroEvidence-aware guide

Kegels look simple: squeeze, hold, release. But a lot of people do them in a way that does nothing—or even makes things worse. If you're wondering "am I doing Kegels wrong?", check these seven signs.

You Hold Your Breath

Sign: you realize at the end of a set that you haven't breathed. Fix: breathe normally the whole time. Try exhaling gently as you squeeze and inhaling as you release.

Your Butt, Thighs or Abs Tighten

Sign: you can see or feel your glutes clench or your belly pull in. Fix: put a hand on your stomach and one on your butt. Both should stay soft. Only the pelvic floor moves—an upward, inward lift.

You Get a Headache, Back Pain or Stomach Ache

Sign: discomfort anywhere after a session. Fix: Kegels shouldn't hurt. Pain usually means breath-holding or recruiting the wrong muscles. Go back to lying down, lower the intensity, and re-find the right muscles.

You Never Fully Relax

Sign: you rush from one squeeze to the next. Fix: the release matters as much as the squeeze. Relax for at least as long as you held—longer if needed. A muscle that can't relax isn't a healthy muscle.

You Do Them While Peeing

Sign: you use the toilet as your regular Kegel time. Fix: stopping your stream is only for identifying the muscles, at most once a month. Regular practice while urinating can disrupt normal bladder emptying.

You Overdo It

Sign: hundreds of reps a day, sore or tense feeling in the pelvis. Fix: more isn't better. A few sets of about 10 reps a day is plenty. Overtraining can leave the pelvic floor tight and tense instead of strong. See Can You Do Too Many Kegels? for more on this.

You Expect Results in a Week

Sign: you quit after 10 days because "nothing changed." Fix: most people notice changes after 6–8 weeks of regular practice. The first month is about learning control; visible results come later.

Bonus: You Only Ever Squeeze

Some people—especially men with a tense pelvic floor—also need reverse Kegels: deliberately relaxing and lengthening the same muscles. If you feel tight, have pelvic pain, or Kegels make symptoms worse, a pelvic floor physical therapist can tell you which one you need.

A Correct Kegel in One Line

Lift inward like you're holding in gas, breathe, hold 3 seconds, release fully 3 seconds—nothing else moves.
