Is Edging Bad for Testosterone? What the Evidence Actually Says

Reviewed September 22, 2026By Kegel Morse HeroEvidence-aware guide

Edging means approaching orgasm and then reducing or stopping stimulation so that climax is delayed. A person may repeat this cycle during masturbation or partnered sex.

Online claims describe edging as either a powerful testosterone booster or a cause of hormonal "exhaustion." Current evidence does not support either extreme.

There is no good evidence that occasional edging causes a meaningful, lasting reduction in testosterone. There is also no convincing evidence that it creates a useful long-term testosterone increase.

What Happens During Edging?

Sexual arousal involves changes in blood flow, nervous-system activity, muscle tension, attention, and hormones. These responses naturally fluctuate throughout sexual activity.

Short-term hormonal changes during arousal or abstinence should not be confused with a lasting improvement or decline in baseline testosterone. A diagnosis of low testosterone requires appropriate symptoms and properly timed blood testing—not an assumption based on sexual habits.

Possible Benefits

Some people use edging to:

  • Learn how arousal builds
  • Practise delaying orgasm
  • Extend a sexual experience
  • Communicate preferences with a partner
  • Explore different rhythms and levels of stimulation

These are subjective or skill-based benefits. They are not proof of hormonal enhancement.

Temporary Aching or Pressure

Prolonged arousal without orgasm may cause temporary genital heaviness or discomfort. This sensation is generally not dangerous and usually fades with time, reduced stimulation, or orgasm.

No one is obligated to provide sexual activity to relieve another person's discomfort.

Skin Irritation or Numbness

Long sessions, high friction, strong vibration, or excessive pressure may irritate the skin or temporarily dull sensation. Use lubricant, change technique, and stop when discomfort begins.

Persistent numbness, bruising, or pain requires medical advice.

Pelvic Floor Tension

Arousal and orgasm involve the pelvic floor. Repeatedly clenching these muscles for a long time may contribute to fatigue or tension in susceptible people.

After a contraction, allow the pelvic floor to release fully. If edging causes pelvic pain, painful ejaculation, urinary urgency, or difficulty relaxing, take a break and consider evaluation by a pelvic floor physiotherapist. Performance Pressure

Edging can become counterproductive if the person becomes preoccupied with timing, duration, or avoiding orgasm. Sexual activity should not feel like a test of discipline.

If the practice causes anxiety, conflict, loss of sleep, soreness, or compulsive behaviour, reduce or stop it.

Does Ejaculation Lower Testosterone?

Research on ejaculation, abstinence, and testosterone is limited and often based on small studies. Temporary fluctuations have been reported, but they do not demonstrate that normal ejaculation lowers testosterone in a clinically meaningful way.

Testosterone is influenced by many factors, including age, sleep, body composition, illness, medication, and testicular or pituitary function. Sexual frequency alone is not a reliable way to diagnose or manage low testosterone.

How to Practise Edging More Comfortably

  • Use sufficient lubricant
  • Reduce pressure and speed before discomfort appears
  • Keep sessions within a comfortable duration
  • Breathe normally instead of bracing the abdomen
  • Avoid continuous maximum-effort Kegels
  • Let the pelvic floor soften during pauses
  • Communicate clearly with a partner and respect consent
  • Stop if you experience pain, numbness, or distress

There is no medically required number of cycles and no health benefit to pushing through discomfort.

How to Support Healthy Testosterone

If your goal is general hormonal health, focus on factors with clearer evidence:

  • Get adequate, consistent sleep
  • Perform regular resistance and aerobic exercise
  • Maintain a sustainable body weight
  • Eat enough overall energy, protein, and dietary fat
  • Limit excessive alcohol
  • Treat sleep apnea and other medical conditions
  • Discuss symptoms and testing with a healthcare professional

Do not take testosterone or "testosterone boosters" without appropriate evaluation. Testosterone therapy has real indications, risks, and monitoring requirements.

When to Seek Medical Advice

Contact a clinician if you have persistent low libido, erectile difficulties, loss of morning erections, infertility concerns, testicular changes, unexplained fatigue, or other symptoms associated with low testosterone.

Seek advice sooner if sexual activity causes recurrent pelvic pain, painful ejaculation, blood in urine or semen, or persistent numbness.

Final Thoughts

Edging is neither a proven testosterone hack nor a known cause of hormonal collapse. For most people, occasional edging is a matter of preference. The main concerns are comfort, irritation, pelvic floor tension, consent, and whether the practice begins to cause distress.

Use it as an optional sexual technique—not as a hormone treatment. Only a qualified clinician can diagnose testosterone deficiency or the cause of persistent sexual symptoms.
