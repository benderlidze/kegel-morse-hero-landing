How To Use Kegel Morse Hero

A step-by-step guide to start pelvic floor training with clear timing.

1

Find the right muscles first

Gently lift and squeeze as if you were stopping the flow of urine or holding in gas, then let go completely. Your belly, buttocks and thighs should stay relaxed and your breathing normal. Use this only to learn the feeling: do not practice by stopping your urine flow regularly.

2

Download and open the app

Kegel Morse Hero is available on the App Store for iPhone and on Google Play for Android. No account is needed: open the app and you can start your first session straight away.

3

Select a Morse code track

Tap the track name (for example, SOS) to choose a built-in track or create your own from any word. Short tracks such as SOS (16 s) or HERO (20 s) are the easiest way to begin.

4

Choose your speed

Select 1x, 2x or 3x. Start with 1x if you are new: holds and rests are longest, which gives you time to squeeze with control and relax fully.

5

Get into position

Lying on your back with your knees bent is the easiest position to start, because the pelvic floor does not have to work against gravity. As control improves, train sitting and then standing.

6

Press play and follow the ring

The ring animation shows when to squeeze and when to relax, and haptic feedback vibrates in sync. A dot is a quick squeeze and release, a dash is a longer hold, and every gap is a full relaxation. Keep breathing throughout.

7

Complete the session

When the track finishes, the session is logged automatically. One to three short tracks is plenty for one session; repeat the track or choose another.

8

Set a reminder and review your progress

Turn on reminders for a time that fits your day, and use the calendar and history to see how consistent you have been over the past weeks.

Tips for effective training

  • Train consistently. Many people aim for 2 to 3 short sessions per day; regular practice matters more than long sessions.
  • Quality beats force: a gentle lift you can control is better than a maximum squeeze.
  • Relaxing fully between signals is part of the exercise, not a break from it.
  • Breathe normally. Never hold your breath or push down.
  • Use haptic feedback and Zen Mode for discreet, eyes-free guidance.
  • Start with short tracks at 1x speed and move to longer tracks, faster speeds and standing positions gradually.
  • Stop and get checked by a doctor or pelvic floor physiotherapist if you feel pain, find it harder to pass urine, or symptoms get worse.
