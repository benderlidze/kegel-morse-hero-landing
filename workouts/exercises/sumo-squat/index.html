Sumo Squat: how to do it (with pictures)

Wide stance, toes out.

WorksInner thighsGlutes

The sumo squat is a wide-stance squat with the toes turned out. It works the inner thighs and glutes, which attach around the pelvis and help support it. It does not train the pelvic floor itself, but it is a good place to practice exhaling as you stand.

How to do it

  1. Wide stance, toes turned out
  2. Knees track over toes
  3. Squeeze up to standing

12 reps

How to know it is working

You should feel your inner thighs and glutes working, most of all as you rise, with your torso upright and knees pointing over your toes. You should not feel knee strain, a hard arch in your lower back or pressure down below. Self-check: if you only feel your front thighs, widen the stance slightly and sit straight down.

Common mistakes

  • Knees collapsing inward. Push your knees out over your toes throughout. If they cave, narrow the stance or turn the toes out less.
  • Bearing down on the way up. Breathe out as you rise and let the pelvic floor lift gently with the effort. Never push down.
  • Going too wide. Pick a stance where your hips feel open and your knees stay comfortable.

Make it easier or harder

Easier: Use a smaller range, hold a chair or squat to a high seat, and cut the set to 6–8 reps.

Harder: Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell between your legs, lower over three seconds, or add a gentle Kegel lift as you stand and fully release at the top.

Who it helps

Useful for lifters warming up for leg day and anyone who wants stronger inner thighs and glutes around the pelvis.

Skip it or get checked first if

Get checked first if you have groin, hip or knee pain, a hernia, recent pelvic or abdominal surgery, prolapse symptoms or leaking when you lift, or are pregnant or postpartum and not yet cleared. Stop if it hurts.

Questions

Is the sumo squat better for the pelvic floor than a regular squat?

Neither trains it directly. The sumo squat loads the inner thighs and glutes more, and many people find the wide stance easier to control. Breathing matters more than stance.

Should I brace or exhale during sumo squats?

For bodyweight reps, a steady exhale as you stand is enough. With heavy loads many lifters brace, but avoid bearing down and let the breath out in a controlled way.
