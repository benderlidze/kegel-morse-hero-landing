Child's Pose: how to do it (with pictures)

Sit back, arms long.

WorksLower backHips

Child's pose is a kneeling rest position that gently stretches your lower back and hips. It does not build strength; its value is relaxation: it gives the pelvic floor a chance to let go, which helps if yours feels tight or you clench without noticing.

How to do it

  1. Knees wide, big toes together
  2. Sit back on your heels
  3. Breathe into your lower back

30–60 s

Let your belly and pelvic floor fully relax here. Do not push into any pain.

How to know it is working

You should feel a mild stretch across your lower back and hips, with a soft belly and slow breathing. You should not feel pinching in your knees, neck strain or an urge to hold your breath. Self-check: as you breathe in, notice a light widening between your sit bones; as you breathe out, let it settle without squeezing.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing your hips down to your heels. Stop where it feels comfortable. A pillow between hips and heels gives the same relaxation without strain.
  • Tensing your belly or pelvic floor. Let the floor carry your weight. Nothing needs to work here, so soften your jaw, belly and pelvic floor.
  • Shallow chest breathing. Breathe slowly into your lower back. A slightly longer exhale helps the pelvic floor release.

Make it easier or harder

Easier: Put a pillow under your hips or chest, or rest your forehead on stacked fists. If kneeling bothers your knees, pad them.

Harder: Walk your hands to one side, then the other, for a side stretch. Hold longer with slower breathing; no squeezes.

Who it helps

Useful for people who sit or drive for hours and anyone whose pelvic floor feels tight or hard to relax after training.

Skip it or get checked first if

Modify it if you have knee pain or recent knee or hip surgery; in pregnancy, widen your knees and support your chest. If you have pelvic pain or pain with sex, a pelvic floor physiotherapist can guide you. Stop if it hurts.

Questions

Is child's pose good for the pelvic floor?

It helps with relaxation rather than strength. The position and slow breathing make it easier for the pelvic floor to let go.

Should I do Kegels in child's pose?

It works better as the relaxation part of your routine. If your pelvic floor tends to be tight, more squeezing is not the fix; letting it fully release is.
