Butterfly Stretch: how to do it (with pictures)

Soles together, lean forward.

WorksInner thighsHips

The butterfly stretch is a seated stretch with the soles of your feet together and your knees falling open. It stretches the inner thighs and hips, right next to the pelvic floor. Its value is relaxation and mobility, not strength.

How to do it

  1. Sit tall, soles of feet together
  2. Let knees drop out
  3. Lean forward from the hips

30–60 s

How to know it is working

You should feel a gentle pull along your inner thighs, and maybe your hips and lower back as you lean forward. You should not feel your back rounding hard, knee pain or breath holding. Self-check: breathe out slowly and notice whether your knees drop a little on their own; if you are fighting the stretch, ease off.

Common mistakes

  • Pushing your knees down with your hands. Let gravity do the work. Muscles tighten against force.
  • Rounding your back to get your head down. Lean forward from the hips with a long spine. A small lean with a straight back stretches more than a slump.
  • Sitting back on your tailbone. Sit on the edge of a cushion or folded towel so your pelvis tips forward and sitting tall takes no effort.

Make it easier or harder

Easier: Move your feet further from your body and sit on a cushion. You can also skip the lean and just sit tall and breathe.

Harder: Bring your heels closer and lean further forward with a long back, using slow exhales to release.

Who it helps

Helpful for people who sit for hours, men and women with tight hips, and anyone who wants an easy evening stretch after Kegels.

Skip it or get checked first if

Skip or modify it if you have groin or knee pain, a hernia or recent groin, hip or abdominal surgery. If your pelvic floor feels tight or sore, a pelvic floor physiotherapist can help you pair stretches with breathing. Stop if it hurts.

Questions

Is the butterfly stretch good for the pelvic floor?

It can support relaxation and hip mobility. The inner thigh muscles attach near the pelvic floor, so easing them can help the pelvic floor let go. It will not strengthen it.

Why are my knees so high in butterfly?

Usually tight inner thighs or hips. Sit on a cushion, move your feet away and give it time.
