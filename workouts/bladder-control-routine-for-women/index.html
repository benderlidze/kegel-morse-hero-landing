Bladder control routine for women: 6 pelvic floor moves

Pelvic floor exercises are a common part of bladder-control programs. This routine covers the basics: finding the muscle, squeezing in different positions and relaxing it. It is not a treatment.

BeginnerA small ball or pillowAt home8 minutes

The workout

Kegel (lying)

10 reps · 3 s hold

Pelvic floor

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat
  2. Squeeze as if stopping your pee and lift up and in
  3. Relax fully between reps

1

Kegel (lying)

Squeeze, hold, relax

10 reps · 3 s hold

WorksPelvic floor

  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat
  2. Squeeze as if stopping your pee and lift up and in
  3. Relax fully between reps

Breathe normally. If your belly, glutes or thighs tense up, the squeeze is too hard: go lighter.

2

Kegel (sitting)

Same squeeze, sitting tall

10 reps · 5 s hold

WorksPelvic floor

  1. Sit tall, feet flat
  2. Squeeze and lift, keep glutes soft
  3. Breathe normally

Do it on a chair, in a car or at a desk: nobody can see it. Keep your shoulders loose.

3

Kegel (standing)

Hardest: fight gravity

10 reps · 5 s hold

WorksPelvic floor

  1. Stand tall, knees soft
  2. Squeeze and lift up and in
  3. Keep your belly and glutes relaxed

The hardest position because gravity works against you. Master the lying version first.

4

Bridge + Ball Squeeze

Bridge with a ball between knees

10 reps

WorksGlutesInner thighsPelvic floor

  1. Ball or pillow between your knees
  2. Squeeze the ball and lift
  3. Hold 2 s at the top

No ball? Use a pillow or a rolled-up towel between your knees.

5

Ball Squeeze

Squeeze a ball between knees

10 reps

WorksInner thighsPelvic floor

  1. Sit tall, ball between knees
  2. Squeeze ball + pelvic floor
  3. Hold 5 s, relax

No ball? A rolled-up towel or a pillow works too.

6

Belly Breathing

Slow breaths into the belly

1–2 min

WorksDiaphragmPelvic floor

  1. One hand on your belly
  2. Inhale: belly rises, floor relaxes
  3. Exhale: gentle lift

Let the pelvic floor soften on the inhale. This is as important as the squeeze.

How to do this routine

  • Do the moves in order, once through.
  • Rest about 30 seconds between moves.
  • If you leak urine or feel pain, see a doctor or a pelvic floor physiotherapist; exercises alone may not be enough.
  • Three to four times a week is a good start; quality beats speed.
