# Do Kegel Exercises Increase Size? The Honest Answer

> **Quick answer:** No. Kegels do not increase penis length or girth. They may improve erection rigidity in some men, which can change appearance without creating new tissue.

No. Kegel exercises do not increase penis length or girth.

They train the pelvic floor muscles. Those muscles can affect erection rigidity, urinary control, and ejaculation, but they do not grow penile tissue.

That distinction matters because a firmer erection may look fuller than an incomplete one. The change is erection quality, not anatomical size.

## Why Do People Think Kegels Increase Size?

The myth usually starts with a real observation.

Some men notice that erections feel firmer after improving pelvic floor control. A firmer erection may reach closer to the person’s normal fully erect size. It can therefore look larger than the softer erection they were comparing it with.

That does not mean new length or girth was created.

A second source of confusion is fitness language. Kegels strengthen muscles, so it is easy to assume they work like curls that make a biceps visibly larger.

The pelvic floor can influence support, pressure, and blood retention during erection. Training it does not add penile tissue or permanently change anatomical length or girth.

## What Do the Male Pelvic Floor Muscles Actually Do?

The pelvic floor forms a muscular support system underneath the bladder and bowel. It also contributes to urinary and sexual function.

Two muscles often discussed in male sexual function are the bulbocavernosus and ischiocavernosus. They participate in the mechanics around erection and ejaculation.

Pelvic floor contractions may help compress veins involved in retaining blood during an erection. Better coordination can therefore support rigidity in some men.

The pelvic floor also contributes to:

- Urinary continence.
- Control after urination.
- Bowel control.
- Ejaculatory function.
- Support during increases in abdominal pressure.
- Relaxation and coordination during urination and bowel movements.

It is not simply a “stronger is always better” system. The muscles also need to relax fully.

## What Does the Research Support?

A randomized controlled trial led by Grace Dorey studied pelvic floor exercises and biofeedback in men with erectile dysfunction. At six months, the published follow-up reported that 40% had regained erectile function within the study’s normal range, 35.5% improved, and 24.5% did not improve.

Those numbers describe a small clinical study, not the odds for any individual reader. The trial studied erectile function. It did not study penis enlargement.

A later [systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32029399/) found that most included studies reported improvement in erectile dysfunction, but the authors also highlighted weak methodology and major differences between training protocols.

That is the accurate summary:

- There is evidence supporting pelvic floor training for some aspects of erectile function.
- The evidence base is not perfect.
- None of this evidence shows permanent increases in penis length or girth.

## Can Better Erection Quality Make You Look Bigger?

Yes, but only in the ordinary sense that a complete erection looks larger than a partial erection.

Suppose a man is not reaching his normal level of rigidity. Improving blood retention or pelvic floor coordination may help him reach a fuller erection.

That can change appearance, angle, and firmness. It does not create a new maximum size.

This is similar to inflating a tire to its intended pressure. The tire looks different, but you did not manufacture a larger tire.

## Can Posture Change the Visible Appearance?

Posture can change angle and visibility, especially when the pelvis is heavily tucked or the lower abdomen obscures part of the base.

Kegels are not a posture program, though. A better standing position may change what is visible, but it does not lengthen the penis.

Avoid turning this into another measurement ritual. Small changes in temperature, stress, arousal, hydration, and measurement technique can create inconsistent numbers.

## What Penis-Enlargement Methods Do Not Work?

Most products marketed for enlargement do not have reliable evidence behind them.

[Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/mens-health/in-depth/kegel-exercises-for-men/art-20045074) states that most advertised enlargement methods do not work and that some can cause permanent damage.

Common examples include:

- Pills and lotions marketed for enlargement.
- Manual “jelqing” or aggressive stretching routines.
- Vacuum pumps used specifically for permanent enlargement.
- Unregulated injections or cosmetic procedures.

Vacuum devices have legitimate medical uses for erectile dysfunction. That does not mean they permanently enlarge the penis. Mayo Clinic specifically notes that there is no proof penis pumps work for enlargement.

Aggressive stretching, excessive vacuum, or unqualified procedures can cause bruising, pain, scarring, reduced sensation, or erection problems.

Do not injure normal tissue while chasing a result that the method has not been shown to produce.

## What Should You Do If Erection Quality Is the Real Concern?

Start by naming the actual problem.

Is the concern:

- Difficulty getting an erection?
- Difficulty keeping it?
- Less rigidity than before?
- Anxiety about normal size?
- Pain, curvature, or a recent change?
- A mismatch between expectations and anatomy?

Erectile dysfunction can have physical, psychological, medication-related, and relationship-related causes. It can also be associated with cardiovascular and metabolic health.

A primary care clinician or urologist can help evaluate a persistent change. That is more useful than adding hundreds of contractions without knowing the cause.

Pelvic floor training may be one part of a broader plan. It is not a universal answer.

More is not better.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do Kegels make the penis thicker?

No. Kegels do not add tissue or permanently increase girth. A firmer erection can look fuller than a softer erection, but that is a difference in rigidity rather than anatomical growth.

### Can Kegels increase flaccid size?

There is no good evidence that pelvic floor exercises permanently increase flaccid length or girth. Flaccid appearance naturally changes with temperature, stress, activity, and blood flow.

### Can Kegels improve erection hardness?

They may help some men, particularly when poor pelvic floor strength or coordination contributes to erectile dysfunction. Research is promising but limited, and erectile dysfunction has many possible causes.

### How long should I try Kegels before judging them?

Do not judge the routine after a few days. Early changes may take several weeks, while meaningful muscular improvement often takes months. Technique and consistency matter more than adding volume.
