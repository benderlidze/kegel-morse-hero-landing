# Kegels Lying Down, Sitting or Standing: Which Is Best?

> **Quick answer:** There is no single best position. Lying reduces the challenge and is often easiest for learning. Sitting makes the movement discreet and practical. Standing adds gravity and prepares the pelvic floor for daily tasks. A useful progression moves from control to function.

## Lying Down: Best for Learning

Lie on your back with knees bent and feet supported. The position reduces the demand of gravity and makes it easier to keep the glutes and thighs quiet.

Use lying when you are new, fatigued, or unable to feel a clean lift in other positions. Do not stay there forever if your goal involves standing, walking, coughing, or lifting.

## Sitting: Best for Awareness and Routine

Sit tall on a firm chair with feet supported. The contact with the chair can improve awareness of lifting away from the seat rather than pushing into it.

Sitting is easy to fit into a workday, but posture does not need to be rigid. Keep the ribs, jaw, abdomen, and glutes relaxed.

## Standing: Best for Functional Progression

Standing is usually harder because the pelvic floor works against gravity and coordinates with the trunk and hips. Begin with soft knees and normal breathing. Leaning against a wall or counter can make the transition easier.

Standing practice is useful because many leaks occur during standing, coughing, carrying, or changing position.

## A Simple Progression

1. Learn a clear lift and release while lying.
2. Repeat it while sitting without bracing.
3. Move to supported standing.
4. Use a gentle pre-contraction before a cough or light lift.

Move back to an easier position if the contraction disappears, you push downward, or other muscles take over. Difficulty in standing does not mean you should squeeze harder.

## When Position Will Not Solve the Problem

Changing position cannot correct an overactive pelvic floor, pain condition, urinary obstruction, or a technique you cannot identify. If symptoms persist or worsen, get an assessment rather than cycling through positions.
