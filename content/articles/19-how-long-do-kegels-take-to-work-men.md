# How Long Do Kegels Take to Work for Men?

Short answer: most men notice changes after 6–8 weeks of regular practice, with bigger improvements over 3–6 months. But what happens in between is what decides whether you get there—so here's a realistic timeline.

## Week 1–2: Learning, Not Results

The first two weeks are about finding the right muscles and doing clean reps. You probably won't feel stronger yet. What you should notice:

- It gets easier to find and isolate the pelvic floor
- You can squeeze without clenching your butt or holding your breath

If this is all you get in two weeks, you're on track.

## Week 3–4: Better Control

Holds start to feel steadier. You can do Kegels sitting or standing, not just lying down. Some people start noticing small changes—for example, less dribbling after urinating.

## Week 6–8: First Real Results

This is where Cleveland Clinic and most pelvic floor specialists say changes typically show up with consistent daily practice. Depending on your goal, that might mean better bladder control, more control during sex, or stronger holds.

## Month 3–6: The Big Gains

Many clinical studies on pelvic floor training for men run for around 12 weeks, and some benefits keep building up to six months. After that, Kegels become maintenance—a few sets a day keep the results.

## What Speeds It Up (or Slows It Down)

- Consistency: daily beats "when I remember" by a mile
- Correct technique: wrong muscles means zero progress
- Full relaxation between reps
- Starting point: weaker muscles take longer
- Age and health: prostate surgery, weight and other factors play a role

## What if Nothing Changes After 8 Weeks?

Don't just push harder. Check for:

1. Wrong muscles. Re-test with the "hold in gas" cue described in [How to Find Your Pelvic Floor Muscles](/articles/how-to-find-pelvic-floor-muscles-men/).
2. Inconsistency. Were you really training daily?
3. A tight pelvic floor. Some men need reverse Kegels (relaxation) rather than more squeezing.

If you've been consistent with correct technique and still see no change—or have pain—talk to a doctor or pelvic floor physical therapist.

## A Simple 8-Week Plan

- Weeks 1–2: 10 reps × 3-second hold, once a day
- Weeks 3–4: 10 reps × 5-second hold, twice a day
- Weeks 5–8: 10 reps × 5–10-second hold plus 10 quick squeezes, 2–3 times a day
